Privacy Policy

Honeycombe Arts Hub (Registered Charity No. 1127371, Ofsted URN EY496668) is the controller of the personal information described here. This notice explains what we collect, why, who sees it, how long we keep it and your rights under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. Questions? Email info@honeycombeartshub.org.uk or call 07932 772905.

When you book with us

To book, a parent or carer creates an account (young adults aged 18+ can register themselves). We ask only for what the activity needs:

  • You: your name, email, mobile, address and at least one emergency contact.
  • Your child: name, date of birth, school or home education, and — for holiday clubs and drop-off sessions — their GP, health needs (allergies, medical conditions, medication), SEND and emotional wellbeing needs, dietary and religious or cultural requirements, and anything about the family you want our safeguarding lead to know.
  • Permissions: your answers about photography, first aid, plasters, emergency treatment and (for older children) going home alone. We record which version of each question you answered and when.
  • Collection: who may collect your child and a collection password. We store the password in a scrambled form that even our staff can't read; staff type in what the collecting adult says and the system checks it.
  • Bookings and payments: what you've booked, attendance (sign-in and sign-out times and who collected), invoices and payments. Card payments are handled by Stripe — we never see or store your card number.
  • SEND support: if you ask us to plan support for your child, what you tell our SEND lead and any plans you upload (such as an EHCP). Uploads are stored privately: only you, our SEND lead and senior staff can open them, and every staff download is logged.
  • One-off events without an account: just an email address, phone number and the number of places.

Why we use it (our lawful bases)

  • Running safe activities for children — including health, SEND and family information — because we have legal duties as a registered childcare provider and a legitimate interest in keeping children safe. Health and safeguarding information about children is used under the substantial public interest condition for safeguarding children (DPA 2018 Schedule 1, paragraph 18). We don't rely on your consent for this, because we need it to look after your child.
  • Your account, bookings and payments — to provide the service you've asked for (contract) and to keep financial records the law requires.
  • Young adults' optional health information — only with your explicit consent, which you can withdraw.
  • Photos — only as you've chosen (including online, internal only, or none). We never publish a child's name with their photo.
  • News about future events — only if you opt in. Every news email has a one-click unsubscribe link, and you can change your choices under “Your data & messages” in your account. Messages about something you've booked (confirmations, changes, invoices) aren't marketing and are always sent.

Who sees it

  • Our staff, each only as much as their role needs. Session staff see health needs through the day's register; family safeguarding information is seen only by our Designated Safeguarding Lead. Every time someone opens health or safeguarding information it's logged.
  • Holiday Activities and Food (HAF): for funded places, BCP Council receives the information the programme requires (such as attendance). This is part of running the funded programme, not something we ask your consent for.
  • Other carers you invite to your account (such as a grandparent) can see your children and bookings, and book, pay and cancel. They can't change your details or see anything you've told our safeguarding lead.
  • Funders receive anonymised numbers only, unless you've said yes to sharing your child's name and age with our funders. We don't publish figures small enough to identify anyone.
  • Service providers who process data for us under contract, with data protection terms: Render hosts our website and stores your information in its Frankfurt (EU) data centre on encrypted disks; Brevo (France) sends our emails; Twilio (USA) sends our texts, under the UK's approved safeguards for transfers; Stripe handles card payments and gets only the booking reference, amount and payer's email — never your child's name or health details; and Backblaze keeps our backups in Amsterdam (EU), encrypted so it can't read them.
  • When the law requires it — for example Ofsted, the police or children's services in a safeguarding situation, or RIDDOR reporting.

We never sell your data, and emails and texts from us never contain health details.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and family details: while you use it. If you delete your account we erase your details after a 14-day cooling-off period.
  • Invoices and payment records: six years (charity and tax law).
  • Accident and injury records: until the child is 25, and always at least 7 years after the accident.
  • Safeguarding records: as long as our safeguarding lead decides is necessary under statutory guidance.
  • Registers: 3 years (longer only if a funded programme's rules require it), after which we keep only anonymous totals. Accounts that never confirm their email address: 7 days.
  • Accounts nobody has used for 3 years: we email you, and delete the account 30 days later unless you sign in.
  • The text of messages we've sent you, and messages sent through our contact form: 24 months. One-off event bookers' contact details: 12 months after the event.
  • When a child turns 18, you can hand their details over to an account of their own (anything you told our safeguarding lead in confidence stays with them and doesn't move); otherwise the details leave your account after 90 days.
  • Backups: 7 days on our server and 35 days off-site, encrypted.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of your data (choose “Request my data” in your account), correct it (most details you can change yourself), ask us to delete it (choose “Delete my account”), object to or restrict how we use it, and withdraw any consent at any time. Some information we must keep by law, as described above; we'll explain if that applies. We reply within one month.

If you're unhappy with how we've handled your information, please tell us first. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.

Cookies

We use only the cookies the site needs to work: one that keeps you signed in to your account (and one for our staff). We don't use advertising or tracking cookies, and our fonts are served from our own site. The map on our contact page only loads from Google if you choose to show it.
